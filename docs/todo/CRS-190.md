# 🎨 CRS-190: Audit production UI consistency, theme and end-to-end usability

Status: open. Owner-requested production requirement (2026-10-01): review the whole UI from design and product-management perspectives before production, covering visual consistency, theme, ease of use and complete flows. This ticket specifies work to perform; it does not certify today's UI. High effort: allocate medium or small audit/remediation children with their own acceptance checks before implementation, or obtain the owner's grade before merging a high-effort implementation. The specification can be added without running the audit.

## Outcome and authority

A person can complete the supported household-record jobs, understand whose information they are seeing and what will happen next, and recover from a mistake without specialist knowledge of the implementation. The interface should feel like one product in both themes, with decoration subordinate to readable medical information and clear actions.

Use the implemented [tokens](../../src/app/tokens.css), shared components and [visual direction](../design/app-design.md) as the starting authority. The [palette JSON](../design/app-palette.json) contains proposals, not automatic permission to replace implemented colors. Reconcile actual inconsistencies against the approved direction; record deliberate changes and their rationale instead of treating every difference as a redesign opportunity.

Use the jobs and owner-stated/inferred personas in [CRS-130](CRS-130.md), then its maintained replacement when available. The audit can start before that document lands, but must label inferred assumptions. Cover the Low-effort self-tracker, Caregiver, High-precision tracker, Records assembler, Receiving clinician, Privacy-sensitive patient, Chronic-condition tracker and Operator; include the Emergency contact's supported printed-output boundary. Medical-grade record integrity means testable ownership, review, source/provenance, correction and recovery clarity, not a certification claim.

## Coverage and review method

1. Inventory every shipped route and user-facing surface on the assessed commit: profile setup/unlock/recovery and management; shell/navigation/theme; Patient; test histories/details/trends; prescriptions and procedures; People and notes; Sources and viewers; Import/review; assistant/Moxie; diagnostics, storage and connection notices; exports and print handoffs. Include menus, action bars, forms, dialogs, drawers, confirmations, history panels, tooltips and toast/status messages. Begin with `src/app/App.tsx`, `components/Shell.tsx` and actual feature components; do not restrict the audit to top-level screenshots.
2. Record each surface's implemented states: first use/empty, loading, populated, long/dense content, selected/expanded, validation error, no matches, disabled/unavailable, active/waiting/failed background work, interrupted save and recovery where supported. Distinguish an absent feature or unsupported state from a passing check. Group shared-component evidence where appropriate, then check representative integrations and every critical flow; document the coverage rationale and any untested combinations.
3. Use a release build with independently fictional data in isolated profiles: at least two people, long/identical names, mixed-person reports, long labels/notes, differing units, source-reported flags, partial/uncertain imports, corrected and archived records, and realistic dense lists. Keep owner data, raw screenshots, logs and runtime databases outside Git. Record commit/build, browser/OS, viewport, theme, input method and fixture scenario so another reviewer can reproduce findings.
4. Review desktop and narrow responsive layouts in Light, Dark and System modes, including a live OS theme change and persisted manual override. Check zoom/reflow, text spacing and reduced motion. Localhost desktop is the adopted release scope; actual phone/device-only qualification remains conditional under [CRS-055](readme.md#crs-055). Viewport simulation does not replace physical-device checks or real assistive-technology evidence.
5. Assign separate design and product reviewers who did not implement the surfaces they assess. Add an accessibility review and a medical-record integrity/operator lens. Each reviewer records evidence, findings and a verdict; consolidate disagreements into explicit decisions. Agent heuristic walkthroughs are labelled as such and never described as user research or proof that real users succeeded. Prepare concise owner walkthroughs only for judgment or physical interaction that cannot be supplied independently; unavailable feedback remains an explicit gap rather than blocking all agent preparation.

## Visual and interaction consistency

Audit typography/hierarchy, spacing, density, alignment, reading widths, color roles, borders/radii, elevation, icons, separators, charts and motion across surfaces and states. Trace repeated styles to shared tokens/components before proposing a new variant. Check contrast on rendered combinations, including banners, selected rows, overlays, hover/focus and semantic states. Document justified exceptions such as original PDF/image colors and charts; do not flatten clinically meaningful differences for cosmetic uniformity.

Equivalent actions need consistent labels, placement, icon meaning and behavior. Sweep all action menus, record headers, cards and dialogs for duplicated actions, competing primary actions, contradictory states, ambiguous icon-only controls and inappropriate disabled actions. Explicitly regress the reported duplicate “Change person” class across every applicable record/detail/review surface. Navigation, back/close/cancel behavior, keyboard shortcuts, selection, sorting/filtering and disclosure patterns should be predictable and preserve the relevant context.

Keep clinical status separate from decoration. Source flags, units, comparator signs, dates/precision, uncertainty, partial scope, current medication status and accepted-versus-proposed information remain legible and unambiguous. Never use color alone, playful branding or a reassuring empty state to imply a medical judgment or complete history. Inspect truncation, wrapping, table overflow and chart-to-table access using actual rendered data; hiding values to make a screen look cleaner fails.

## Product journeys and ease of use

For each journey, state the persona/job, starting state, intended result, steps and decision points, what is visible before a consequential action, completion feedback, escape/recovery path and proof of the resulting record state. Count clicks/actions, prompts, repeated entry, navigation reversals and context switches; identify avoidable work without removing necessary clinical review. Set journey-specific action expectations before the walkthrough and compare proposed improvements with the same fixture and build baseline. Do not use arbitrary click quotas that reward skipping necessary review. Do not set model-time pass/fail limits or confuse waiting on a provider with app responsiveness.

- **Start and return:** create/unlock the intended profile, save/use recovery material, understand lock/session expiry and connection loss, and return to the intended work safely. Recovery instructions distinguish profile access from record recovery and never leak private information on locked screens.
- **File a new report:** upload, understand receipt versus processing versus review readiness, find and resolve questions, verify source evidence and person, accept the intended selection once, and locate the saved records. Bulk actions disclose scope and partial results; repeated clicks/retries do not create misleading confirmations or duplicate actions.
- **Catch up on a backlog:** navigate a large growing review queue, search/filter, handle duplicates/reuploads and mixed-person reports, and understand pending, failed, incomplete and exceptional work. Check that progress, waiting reasons, cancellation and resumption are truthful and that supported foreground browsing/chat/review remains usable.
- **Manage family records:** distinguish the signed-in profile, viewed person/filter and accepted record owner; inspect Self and non-Self people; resolve similar names; change an assignment with clear scope and correction history. A filter must never look like a write, and a write must never look like a harmless view change.
- **Understand a result:** find a result, compare history/trends with explicit units and source flags, inspect its original page, and return without losing place. Prescriptions/orders/current status and procedures must not be presented as interchangeable clinical events.
- **Correct or organize:** edit supported notes/person details, correct an accepted record, change ownership, archive/restore and inspect history. Distinguish draft discard, archive/restore, accepted correction, ownership reversal and permanent profile deletion by consequence and available recovery. Show what will change and what remains evidence; preserve valid input on recoverable failure, prevent silent overwrite on stale versions, and provide an appropriate undo or forward-correction route.
- **Prepare a handoff:** generate supported visit/provider output, understand person/scope/exclusions and completeness disclosures, preview/check the exported artifact, and distinguish patient assertions from provider originals and unaccepted AI proposals. Inspect the receiving clinician's experience; do not imply that packet exclusions or condition features still in the backlog already exist.
- **Ask for help and diagnose:** use assistant/Moxie in context, understand proposed changes and required review, recover from unavailable providers/errors, and download useful diagnostics with clear sensitive-metadata and missing-history explanations. Operational detail belongs where it helps a decision, not throughout ordinary record flows.

## Accessibility and resilient states

Target applicable [WCAG 2.2 Level A and AA success criteria](https://www.w3.org/TR/WCAG22/) across complete supported processes, with a criterion-to-evidence map and documented applicability. Include keyboard reachability/order and absence of traps; visible/unobscured focus and restoration; accessible names/roles/values; headings/landmarks; dialog semantics; status/error announcements; form labels and helpful validation; target size/drag alternatives; color-independent meaning and contrast; zoom/reflow; accessible authentication; and consistent help. Use automated checks plus manual keyboard and screen-reader walkthroughs on the chosen release browser/OS. An automated score alone does not establish accessibility or a blanket conformance claim.

Exercise error, empty, loading, disabled and partial states intentionally. Explain the problem in plain language, retain context and safe input, offer a concrete next action, and distinguish “nothing found,” “not loaded,” “not reviewed” and “not supported.” Check long-running actions, retry/cancel, stale selections/versions, navigation during save, lock/profile changes and interrupted uploads without suggesting that unsaved or incomplete information is durable. Verify that focus, scroll position and asynchronous announcements do not make recovery harder.

## Proposed execution divisions

Allocate independently sized children before starting: (1) surface/state inventory, fictional fixtures and shared visual/theme review; (2) patient/caregiver core journeys and medical-record semantics; (3) accessibility, responsive behavior and interruption/recovery walkthroughs; (4) output/operator handoffs and consolidated release review. Assign overlapping checks once and share evidence. Remediation belongs in the existing owning tickets or focused new fixes; each child preserves this coverage and the final release gate. These are proposed boundaries, not allocated IDs or completed work.

## Findings, delivery and production gate

Keep findings in the CRS item they concern, reusing existing tickets instead of maintaining a parallel report backlog. Each finding needs a reproducible scenario, affected persona/job and surface/state, expected versus observed behavior, evidence location, severity/rationale, proposed fix, owning ticket, effort/impact sizes and regression acceptance. Keep subjective preferences separate from demonstrated task failure. Group repeated causes into shared-component fixes and recheck their integrations.

- **Blocker:** wrong-person or false saved/reviewed/completeness cues, obscured clinical facts, privacy exposure, inaccessible or impossible critical job, or a recovery/destructive-action failure that risks lost work or records. Fix and revalidate before release; a numeric grade cannot average these away.
- **Major:** substantial confusion, repeated work or inconsistent behavior in a core job. Fix before release, or obtain an explicit owner disposition with an alternative/workaround and follow-up; an unanswered question is not approval.
- **Minor:** cosmetic or nonblocking friction. Prioritize with the normal backlog score and document any deferral; do not hold unrelated safe work for personal taste.

Deliver a reproducible surface/state and journey coverage record, consolidated design/product/accessibility verdicts, scoped implementation tickets, and current rerun evidence after fixes. Update maintained design/component guidance only with adopted implemented decisions; leave proposals and findings in the work list. Coordinate [CRS-130](CRS-130.md) personas, [CRS-124](readme.md#crs-124) People review, [CRS-139](CRS-139.md) review UI, [CRS-140](CRS-140.md)/[CRS-141](CRS-141.md) recovery and edits, [CRS-055](readme.md#crs-055) devices, [CRS-073](readme.md#crs-073)/[CRS-150](CRS-150.md) printed packets, and [CRS-154](CRS-154.md)/[CRS-194](CRS-194.md) diagnostics/large imports. Existing functional/device/provider gates remain independently required.

Audit completion requires current evidence for every critical supported journey, explicit treatment of every inventoried surface/state, a scoped owning ticket and regression acceptance for every blocker, a disposition for every major/minor finding, and independent passing review against this specification. The audit may complete with findings; production remains blocked until all launch blockers are fixed and revalidated and major findings have the required disposition. Any required manual/owner evidence still absent is unverified. The enduring [CRS-061](readme.md#crs-061) release gate retains UI/UX requalification and outstanding launch-blocking findings even after this audit ticket is deleted or split. Retirement follows the normal ticket/remainder policy and does not turn a successful audit report into proof that all fixes shipped.
