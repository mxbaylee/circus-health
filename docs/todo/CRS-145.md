# 🆘 CRS-145: Explore emergency-contact break-glass access

Status: open, exploration, lower priority. Owner-led. Opened during backlog grooming on 2026-09-30. It feeds [CRS-084](CRS-084.md)'s risk list. Line numbers were checked at `1e6fa50`; re-verify before editing.

## Decision and why

Owner, 2026-09-30: the owner likes the idea and wants it explored, at lower priority.

The idea: an emergency contact chooses "Log in as emergency contact" and receives an emailed one-time code. The access is logged, and the patient sees it clearly the next time they sign in.

- **Emergency contact** ([CRS-130](CRS-130.md)): reads in a hurry and never uploads. Today they cannot reach anything in the app.
- **Caregiver:** the people they manage have no logins, so the caregiver is often also the person someone else would need to reach in an emergency.
- **Privacy-sensitive patient:** any emergency access must be something they chose in advance, limited to what they chose, and visible to them afterwards.

## Where it stands

- **Emergency Contact is a People tag and a care-team role ✅**, not an account (`docs/features/people-tags.md:3`; `src/shared/person-care.ts:5`; `careTeam.emergencyContactId` in `src/shared/api.ts:234`). In a provider packet, active care and emergency contacts share only names, roles, phone or email and a scheduling URL (`src/server/NOTE-EXPORTS.md:13`).
- **Nobody but the profile's own unlock methods can decrypt it ✅.** The profile data key encrypts the vault, and each unlock method (recovery key, passkey via WebAuthn PRF) protects a separate wrapped copy of that key (`docs/security/profile-encryption.md:28-30`). An ordinary WebAuthn signature or an email code does not decrypt anything.
- **The app sends no email ✅.** No SMTP or mail-service dependency exists in `src` or `deploy`.
- **Reachability:** the supported installation is local Docker Compose on the owner's machine, bound to loopback by default (`docs/security/model.md`, A3). An app on a closed laptop cannot be reached at all. Hosting is [CRS-083](CRS-083.md).

## Constraints to record

- **Identity is not decryption.** An emailed code can show that someone controls the contact's email address. It cannot unlock the profile. The patient must grant a separate key in advance, the same idea as [CRS-081](CRS-081.md)'s job-scoped key, possibly covering only a limited emergency summary rather than the whole archive.
- **Reachability depends on CRS-083.** Without hosting, or a device that is on and reachable, the flow cannot run.
- **Email needs an outside service** the app does not use today, with its own credentials, cost and privacy review. The code is sent to a person, so it leaves the local boundary.
- **Logging must work before unlock.** The access record the patient sees next time is the same problem as [CRS-146](CRS-146.md): it is written while the patient's profile is locked, so it cannot go into the encrypted profile and must contain no health data.
- **Low-tech alternative available now:** a pre-prepared emergency packet, printed or saved by the patient, using the existing provider packet ([note exports](../../src/server/NOTE-EXPORTS.md)).

## Requirements

This is exploration. The deliverable is a written proposal, not an implementation.

1. Compare the options in the owner's terms: the pre-printed emergency packet; a patient-granted emergency key over a limited summary; the full emailed-code flow. For each, say what the emergency contact can see, what must exist in advance, what the patient sees afterwards, and what it depends on (CRS-083 hosting, an email service, CRS-146 logging).
2. For the key option, describe how the patient grants, limits, reviews and revokes it, and what it covers, without weakening one-person-per-profile or the existing unlock methods.
3. Add each option's new risks to CRS-084's list with T-shirt likelihood and impact (S/M/L), per the owner's 2026-09-30 decision there. For example: a phished code, a stale grant after the contact changes, a hosted service reachable from the internet.
4. Keep the proposal free of health data and use fictional people, for example "Robin Lane" as the patient and "Sam Ortiz" as the contact.

## Owner decisions needed

- Which option, if any, to build after the proposal.
- What an emergency summary should contain (for example allergies, current prescriptions, conditions from [CRS-085](CRS-085.md), care contacts), and whether the patient picks items.
- Whether the patient is notified immediately, which also needs a channel the app does not have, or only at next sign-in, as proposed.

## Completion

The proposal is written into this ticket or a design note, its risks are added to CRS-084, and the owner has chosen. Building a chosen option becomes its own ticket with its own independent grade, per `AGENTS.md`. Then delete this ticket.
