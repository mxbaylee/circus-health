# CRS-133: Human attribution despite an incorrect printed DOB

Status: open. Owner-approved decision, 2026-09-29; implementation specification. This behavior is not implemented by documenting it here.

## Decision and why

Allow the person to assign one reviewed report to Self or another Person despite a verified printed DOB that conflicts with that person's saved DOB. A discrepancy continues to block automatic attribution. The exception is an explicit human assertion about ownership, not a correction to the original or saved demographics. See [CRS-126 D5](CRS-126.md#d5-identity-evidence-human-decisions-and-incorrect-printed-dobs-are-different-facts).

This supersedes the current rule that a verified conflicting DOB always requires another destination. That rule protects against wrong-person assignment but treats accurate transcription as proof that the provider's value is true. The revised rule preserves the warning and requires an informed, auditable decision rather than forcing a permanent veto.

- **Caregiver:** can file a parent's or child's genuinely misprinted report without creating a duplicate Person or changing a correct profile DOB.
- **Low-effort self-tracker:** answers once for the applicable report, not once per clinical result. An already reviewed unchanged discrepancy should not become a recurring prompt.
- **High-precision tracker:** keeps useful source evidence and correct attribution through reload/recovery while preventing one incorrect DOB from becoming a future matching rule.
- **Records assembler:** can show the provider's original assertion and the person's later attribution decision separately; the app never represents the provider as having amended the original.

These persona descriptions come from [CRS-130](CRS-130.md); their documented evidence levels remain unchanged. This is a product judgment, not a claim that user confirmation proves identity beyond doubt.

Medical-records design precedents support traceability: [ONC describes matching across multiple demographic fields](https://healthit.gov/standards-and-technology/patient-identity-and-patient-record-matching/); [HHS describes amendment requests and statements of disagreement](https://www.hhs.gov/hipaa/for-individuals/medical-records/index.html); [FHIR provenance records the actors, versions and sources involved in a change](https://hl7.org/fhir/R4/provenance.html). These references support preserving the source and attributing the new assertion. They do not mandate this exact UI, certify identity or mean that the app updates a provider's medical record.

## Review experience

1. Show the exact report/original context, printed name and DOB, selected person and saved DOB together. Keep the original reachable at the affected location. A discrepancy may mean the wrong person, so the exception is never preselected or inferred by the model.
2. Distinguish three actions: correct an extraction that does not match the original; edit an incorrect saved DOB through the existing demographic workflow; or explicitly assert that the printed DOB is incorrect for this report's intended owner. The last action must not modify either date to make the comparison pass.
3. Offer the specific confirmation **“This report belongs to [person]; its printed birth date is incorrect.”** Keep choose-another-person and leave-for-review paths available. Capture a structured reason with that action and optional explanatory text; require no repetitive free-text statement per result. Use the same flow for Self and another Person under the profile's existing authorization.
4. Display the resulting state as human-assigned despite a DOB discrepancy, with the original and decision history accessible. Never relabel the mismatching dates as an evidenced DOB match. A stable reviewed exception may cover later records from that same unchanged report under the scope rules below; it is not a bulk waiver for an entire uploaded file.
5. Clinical values still require their ordinary explicit approval. The exception does not approve OCR/source text, resolve other identity questions, activate medication use or accept records. Name learning, when appropriate, remains the separately visible printed-name assertion in normal confirmation, subject to CRS-119; the wrong DOB never becomes a known identity value.

## Scope, authority and persistence

Extend the shared report confirmation contract with an explicit typed printed-DOB exception. Retain backward-compatible decoding: an older ordinary confirmation has no implied exception. The server records authenticated actor context, timestamp, chosen person, structured reason, optional note, the exact conflicting source DOB facts/locations, saved DOB snapshot, original fingerprint/member, report/printed-subject boundary and reviewed scope/dependencies. Do not claim a caregiver's legal representative status merely because they operate the profile. The receipt describes what this authorized app user asserted.

Validate the original, the chosen active person, current relevant identity facts and exact report scope before confirmation and again at clinical acceptance. The exception resolves only the disclosed DOB discrepancy. Ungrounded/missing subject evidence, ambiguous source boundaries, unresolved competing person claims, explicit refusals and other unreviewed contradictions still require their normal resolution. A generic confirmation or an edited clinical mapping cannot stand in for this typed action.

The current policy must distinguish: verified printed DOB evidence; an explicit human attribution exception; and the saved demographic DOB. Retain all three separately. Apply the same checks in the chooser, feed, individual/bulk acceptance and accepted-ownership correction. No model tool, legacy client, background worker or forged receipt may silently manufacture the exception.

Reuse requires the same retained original/member, report and printed-subject boundary, selected person and reviewed discrepancy to remain applicable. Candidate membership growth alone must not make the same unchanged report ask again, but current identity receipts still require exact candidate/version/record targets: preserving their original applicability through membership growth does not authorize new targets. Implement a durable report-decision extension for later exact occurrences, linked to the original exception decision and pinning their current versions, boundary and source/person facts. Recheck effective authority and absence of new contradictions before appending it. Keep the original identity and clinical acceptance receipts immutable. Reuse existing durable transaction/scope helpers where appropriate, without assuming the later-member authority already exists. Extension is idempotent for the same decision and targets and grants no clinical acceptance.

Changed source facts, new contradictory identity claims, a different report/member, a changed selected person's DOB, or superseded/refused authority require fresh review rather than extension. An unrelated edit to the person must not invalidate an otherwise applicable decision merely to create more prompts. A new re-export is not automatically covered just because its name and erroneous DOB resemble an earlier report.

Keep the exception report-scoped; no incorrect-DOB alias, global ignore-DOB setting or cross-report exception learning. After an ownership correction, an exception for the former person is not valid for the destination. Reuse CRS-119's current-authority/supersession handling so immutable old receipts cannot revive a corrected attribution. A repeated identical retained original may reuse its still-applicable decision after the same current checks; a distinct report cannot.

Journal the small exception receipt and affected pending assignment atomically, with stable operation identity, exact replay and changed-payload rejection. Survive lost responses, restart and rebuild without duplicating confirmation or rewriting prior evidence. Preserve current profile/session authorization at confirmation and acceptance; Stop or scoped import authority never grants an unattended identity decision.

Record views, source inspection, history and app-generated structured exports must distinguish the user-attributed owner from the printed discrepancy and reference the decision. Original-file download stays byte-for-byte identical. Do not silently alter clinical values, printed age or age-dependent reference intervals to make them agree with the selected DOB; any clinical correction remains a separate reviewed action. Undo or changing the person appends a new reviewed decision and invalidates conflicting current authority without deleting the old receipt.

## Regression scenarios and their reasons

Use independently fictional fixtures and preserve paired before/after expectations:

- A correctly read labelled DOB conflicts with Self, then with another Person. Preview and individual/bulk acceptance stay blocked without an exception; explicit reviewed assignment permits normal clinical approval. **Why:** a mismatch is evidence demanding review, not permission for automatic matching or a permanent veto on human correction.
- Report A's exception does not unblock conflicting report B, even on the same original or with the same printed name and DOB. Later members of unchanged A receive an exact durable extension, remain clinically unaccepted and do not mutate A's earlier receipts; repeated extension/rebuild does not duplicate authority. Changed identity evidence blocks extension. **Why:** avoiding repeated answers for one report must not turn a local exception into a false global identity or imply an old receipt covered unseen targets.
- The app misread the DOB, the profile DOB is wrong, or the provider printed a wrong DOB. Exercise the three distinct correction routes. Original bytes and saved demographics remain unchanged by the attribution exception. **Why:** transcription, demographic truth and ownership are different assertions.
- Model-only DOB hints remain advisory; compatible unlabelled banners retain their existing limited name-only behavior; ambiguous labelled dates keep their interpretation flow. **Why:** adding a path for verified printed conflicts must not strengthen weaker evidence or collapse CRS-132's distinction.
- New DOB evidence, changed report scope, changed selected DOB, destination archival, explicit refusal, forged exception payload and cross-profile requests cannot reuse old approval. Unrelated person edits do not force another decision. **Why:** review follows the facts that mattered, rather than either trusting stale authority or asking again for irrelevant changes.
- A normal/legacy confirmation, wrong-person receipt or edited clinical value never resolves this conflict. An exception for an old owner cannot survive reassignment as authority for the new owner. **Why:** only the deliberate human action authorizes the narrow exception.
- Concurrent updates, lost confirmation response, same-operation replay, process crash and rebuild preserve one decision and require no clinical publication at confirmation. **Why:** reliability must not create stronger authority or duplicate acceptance.
- The caregiver can see who was assigned, what was printed and why it was disputed in review/history and structured export; the original remains unchanged. **Why:** a later clinician or records assembler must not mistake the user's assertion for a provider correction.

Run formatting, typechecks, focused policy/confirmation/acceptance/recovery tests and mounted/encrypted browser journeys. An independent capable reviewer must grade the implementation, not merely the existence of this specification.
