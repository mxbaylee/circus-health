# 🧾 CRS-156: Restore provable owners of historical raw assertions in printed packets

Status: open. Remainder of CRS-147 after safe, person-scoped packet selection. Current accepted imports retain `report_subject` evidence even for the default Self owner, but earlier accepted raw allergy, condition, visit and immunization assertions can lack that row. The PDF and evidence companion now give a generic notice when otherwise eligible raw assertions have no unique verified report subject; they do not print those assertions or reveal their title, count, contents or possible owner. See [note exports](../../src/server/NOTE-EXPORTS.md).

## Requirements

1. Identify historical raw assertions whose subject can be proven from retained accepted mapping or exact report-scoped identity decisions. Do not infer Self from missing person evidence, raw JSON names, a shared source file or a clinician/relative `source` mention. Mixed-family exports and conflicting decisions must remain unassigned until reviewed.
2. Provide a review path for unresolved historical assertions. Show the retained original and proposed person assignment to the profile owner before any accepted change. Preserve originals and accepted versions as durable authority, and keep writes proportional to changed assertions.
3. Once ownership is proven or reviewed, make the assertion available in the correct person's packet and remove the generic omission notice only when no eligible ambiguous assertions remain. Test an older profile and cache-loss rebuild, including Self, managed Person, a clinician mention, conflicting ownership and a mixed-family export.
4. Keep the packet disclosure nonidentifying until ownership is established. Update maintained import, storage and packet documentation with the supported restoration path and its limits.

Which retained historical decision formats carry enough exact member and owner context to restore ownership without asking the user again? Inventory them against current code and independently fictional migrated fixtures before designing a backfill.
