## Personas known now

Each is marked by where it came from. Owner-stated facts are dated; inferred ones name their source.

- **Low-effort self-tracker** (inferred, CRS-129). Keeps their own records; wants to upload and approve with as few questions as possible. Unnecessary prompts are alert fatigue and lower safety. Still has a goal (owner, 2026-09-30): a successful checkup with accurate data. Low effort is how they want to get there, not a lower bar for accuracy.
- **Caregiver** (inferred, People tags and CRS-126 scenario 14; owner facts 2026-09-30). Manages a parent's or child's records, sometimes in separate profiles. The people they manage have no logins of their own (owner, 2026-09-30), and profiles are never shared, so "who checked what" is always the caregiver; "when" still matters. To show records to someone else, they print the patient's PDF output.
- **Records assembler** (inferred, CRS-129). Gathers records for a clinician, second opinion or insurance dispute. Needs to show that a record came from source text that was actually checked.
- **High-precision tracker** (owner, 2026-09-29). Uploads a large backlog from about fifteen sources, down to an eyeglass prescription, then every new medical record as it arrives. May take the easy route and re-upload a full clinical-history export to pick up one new note from their doctor.
- **Receiving clinician** (owner, 2026-09-30). Reads a provider packet and its evidence JSON ([note exports](../../src/server/NOTE-EXPORTS.md)) and never opens the app. For every fact they need to know whose it is, whether a person approved it or it is only the AI's reading of the original, whether anything is missing, and whether it was corrected: by whom, when, and whether the correction is the patient's own assertion or came from a provider. A record filed under the wrong person is a patient-safety event for them, not an inconvenience.

- **Operator** (inferred; record-integrity requirement owner-stated, 2026-10-01). ## Medical-records practice

A lens for the receiving clinician's checks and for any ticket that changes ownership, correction or export. These are general record-keeping norms summarized from memory, not re-checked against their current texts; the persona document should cite each source it relies on.

- **Patient matching uses more than a name.** ONC patient-matching work names generational suffixes (Jr./Sr.) and twins among the common causes of mismatches.
- **An amendment the patient requests is recorded as theirs.** Under HIPAA's amendment right, a provider may decline a change, and the patient's statement of disagreement is then kept with the record rather than overwriting it.
- **Provenance is pinned to a version.** FHIR Provenance points at the specific version of the resource it describes, so a later change does not rewrite what an earlier fact was based on.
- **The original stays unchanged.** Corrections are additions that reference what they correct.
- **A misfiled record is corrected forward.** Moving a document to the right patient includes telling anyone who already received it under the wrong one.

## Requirements

The document is a jobs-to-be-done analysis (owner, 2026-09-30). Each job, such as "file one new result", "catch up on a whole history", "hand records to a new clinician" or "correct who a report belongs to", says which personas it serves, and a persona may need different things from the same job. This replaces [CRS-126](CRS-126.md#scenarios)'s per-situation scenario table as the organizing structure: each CRS-126 scenario and each unwritten one maps to a job, and the scenario checks move under their job. Tickets then cite a job and persona by name.

For each persona, the document gives:

- Who they are, what they upload and how often, how much prompting they tolerate, and what a failure costs them.
- The jobs they need done, and what they need differently in each.
- Checks of today's behavior, each marked ✅ with its evidence inline (a test name or `file:line`) or ⚠️ unverified with what would settle it. Record where the app and a persona's need disagree rather than resolving it silently; a disagreement becomes a ticket or joins an existing one.
- Which facts are owner-stated and which are inferred, so an inferred one can be confirmed or dropped later.

Checks to start from. Those marked ✅ were verified during backlog grooming on 2026-09-30, with the evidence inline; the rest stay unverified until the document shows its evidence:

- High-precision tracker: re-uploading a full-history export to get one note reads every page (CRS-126 scenario 4), clears already-saved records in one action and surfaces only the new note. What that costs in model requests, pages read and paid inference for a large export, and whether the person is told before it starts.
- High-precision tracker: an eyeglass prescription has somewhere to go — which record kind it becomes, or whether it is kept only as a document.
- High-precision tracker: page approval stays workable across a large, growing corpus once it gates acceptance (CRS-129): the count of unapproved pages, and whether a re-upload's already-approved pages need approving again.
- Low-effort self-tracker: a single lab report raises no question when Self's name and birth date match (CRS-126 scenario 1).
- Caregiver: records never cross profiles, and an import in one profile continues while they use another (CRS-126 scenario 14).
- ✅ Caregiver and self-tracker: a report that matched Self on name alone, with no printed birth date, shows "(you?) · Review" rather than "(you) · Change" before bulk approval (`docs/import/identity-review.md:9`, [report identity review](../import/identity-review.md#automatic-matches-and-human-confirmation)). - Operator: a representative import on a small laptop, in counts (requests, rereads, bytes hashed, durable writes) and interactive requests still answered (CRS-083).
- Privacy-sensitive patient: one record can be left out of one provider's packet without hiding it from the patient's own view.

- Emergency contact: what they can reach today without the patient's login (expected: nothing but a printed packet; CRS-145).
