# CRS-130: Write the personas, with verified checks against today's app

Status: open.

Write a persona document, `docs/design/personas.md`, from what is known now. Each persona says who they are and what they need, and lists checks of how the current app serves them, each one verified against code or tests or marked unverified. Tickets then cite personas by name instead of re-inferring them, as [CRS-129](CRS-129.md) had to.

## Where it stands

There is no persona document. The closest material is the scenario table in [CRS-126](CRS-126.md#scenarios) — fourteen stories with checks, written per situation rather than per person — and the persona list in [CRS-129](CRS-129.md#personas), inferred from People tags and review handoff. CRS-126 also lists unwritten scenarios: a family's records mixed in one archive, an imaging report arriving with its images, and a structured portal export.

## Personas known now

Each is marked by where it came from. Owner-stated facts are dated; inferred ones name their source.

- **Low-effort self-tracker** (inferred, CRS-129). Keeps their own records; wants to upload and approve with as few questions as possible. Unnecessary prompts are alert fatigue and lower safety.
- **Caregiver** (inferred, People tags and CRS-126 scenario 14). Manages a parent's or child's records, sometimes in separate profiles, and hands pages off to family. Needs to know who checked what, and when.
- **Records assembler** (inferred, CRS-129). Gathers records for a clinician, second opinion or insurance dispute. Needs to show that a record came from source text that was actually checked.
- **High-precision tracker** (owner, 2026-09-29). Uploads a large backlog from about fifteen sources, down to an eyeglass prescription, then every new medical record as it arrives. May take the easy route and re-upload a full clinical-history export to pick up one new note from their doctor.

## Requirements

For each persona, the document gives:

- Who they are, what they upload and how often, how much prompting they tolerate, and what a failure costs them.
- The CRS-126 scenarios that apply, and any scenario they need that is not written yet.
- Checks of today's behavior, each marked ✅ with its evidence inline (a test name or `file:line`) or ⚠️ unverified with what would settle it. Record where the app and a persona's need disagree rather than resolving it silently; a disagreement becomes a ticket or joins an existing one.
- Which facts are owner-stated and which are inferred, so an inferred one can be confirmed or dropped later.

Checks to start from, each unverified until the document shows its evidence:

- High-precision tracker: re-uploading a full-history export to get one note reads every page (CRS-126 scenario 4), clears already-saved records in one action and surfaces only the new note. What that costs in time and paid inference for a large export, and whether the person is told before it starts.
- High-precision tracker: an eyeglass prescription has somewhere to go — which record kind it becomes, or whether it is kept only as a document.
- High-precision tracker: page approval stays workable across a large, growing corpus once it gates acceptance (CRS-129): the count of unapproved pages, and whether a re-upload's already-approved pages need approving again.
- Low-effort self-tracker: a single lab report raises no question when Self's name and birth date match (CRS-126 scenario 1).
- Caregiver: records never cross profiles, and an import in one profile continues while they use another (CRS-126 scenario 14).
- Caregiver and self-tracker: a report that matched Self on name alone, with no printed birth date, shows "(you?) · Review" rather than "(you) · Change" before bulk approval ([CRS-111](CRS-111.md), name-only matches).
- Records assembler: an accepted record shows the source page it came from and whether that page's text was approved (CRS-129).
