# 🖋️ CRS-129: Record the verified source state behind each proposal

Status: open.

Record, separately from staleness, whether the source text a proposal or accepted clinical record was built from had been checked by a person: which revision, by whom, when, and whether that check still covers today's text.

## Where it stands

Source text has revisions. A _material_ revision changes the text; a _confirmation_ revision records that a person checked a page without changing it. Confirmations are their own revisions in history (`src/server/intake-source-text.ts`), but `sourceTextConfirmationOnly` (`src/server/intake-source-text-dependencies.ts`) keeps the intake's source pin (`src/server/intake-source-pin.ts`, record `intake_source_pin:v1:<intake>`) on the last material revision — a confirmation that resolves an open uncertainty (`resolvedIssueIds`) is excluded from that check and counts as material instead, because it changes what the text claims. Proposals copy the pin (`sourceTextRevisionId`, `sourceTextDependencyToken` in `src/server/intake.ts`); measured proposals additionally pin the pages/spans and context actually read, while unknown coverage retains the broad pin (`assertCurrentProposalSourceText` in `src/server/intake-source-text-dependencies.ts`). Confirming a page therefore never stales existing work. That is correct for staleness: [import processing](../import/processing.md#durable-evidence-and-review-boundaries) documents that "unchanged-text inspection preserves the material dependency."

The gap: nothing records whether the text a proposal or accepted record was built from had actually been checked, which revision recorded that check, who did it or when. Each source-text revision keeps a `review` event with an `actor` and `at` timestamp, and each page carries its own `inspected` flag, but the flag reflects only the current revision's state — reconstructing "was this page confirmed before or after the proposal was created" means walking the revision chain by hand, and is weak under concurrent tabs where a confirmation and a proposal can race.

This item's origin is a review finding (R10): a test in `src/server/test/intake-source-integration.test.ts` ("source corrections stale original/proposal review; identical fresh payload needs a new candidate review") had asserted that a fresh proposal's `sourceTextRevisionId` moves to a confirmation's revision id. That contradicted the pin-to-material rule above and was corrected in `bbe4075` (the test now asserts the proposal pins to the last material revision, and that each confirmation chains to its parent). The finding surfaced the same gap this ticket addresses — the test was reaching for a way to say "this proposal knows its text was checked," and the pin is the wrong place to put that.

Not implemented ✅ (checked 2026-09-30): there is no page-approval gate on clinical acceptance today.

## General practice this design follows

Medical-records practice keeps content version and attestation as separate facts; this is not a regulatory requirement for this app, but it is worth following. Staleness follows content only. An attestation — who checked a page, when, and what content it covers — is an audit fact tied to a specific content version, not a new content version. It stops covering content once that content changes, but its record is kept rather than discarded. Derived records point at the content version they were made from and can show both the verification state at creation and the current state, the way a lab result can be marked preliminary until verified. FDA 21 CFR Part 11's audit-trail and signature requirements link an attestation to the specific record version it covers, and FHIR's `Provenance` resource targets a specific resource version rather than "the resource" in general; both are reference points for the shape of this design, not requirements this app must meet.

## Personas

Personas are defined in [CRS-130](CRS-130.md); this ticket cites them by name.

- **Low-effort self-tracker.** Confirming a page must not re-review proposals already made from it. Churn here is alert fatigue, which lowers safety. Their goal is still a successful checkup with accurate data, so a one-pass bulk approval must still put the text in front of them.
- **Caregiver.** The people a caregiver manages have no logins, so "who checked" is always the caregiver; "when" still matters.
- **Records assembler** and **receiving clinician.** Need to show that a record came from source text a person actually checked.
- **High-precision tracker.** Approves pages across a large, growing corpus and needs to see which pages are still unapproved.

Pinning staleness to material content, whether measured pages/spans or the conservative whole source, serves the self-tracker but gives the others nothing to point at. Moving the pin to confirmations, as the R10 test wrongly expected, would serve them but bring back review churn. Keeping both facts, separately, serves all of them.

## Owner decisions, 2026-09-29

- **Verified means the page's text was approved.** A person approving a page (**Approve section**, or **Select all shown → Approve text sections**) says its retained text matches the original, so the page is identified and searchable. The purpose is coverage — no unknown unknowns, so a result is never read and then never shown — not a span-level or clinical check.
- **Approval gates acceptance.** A clinical record cannot be accepted until the pages it drew on are approved. The person gets two chances to catch an error: the text, then the record.
- **Records accepted before this lands** are not reconstructed from history. Their pages show as not approved and are offered as review work; approving text is quick next to AI reading, so this gives the person something useful to do while reading runs.

## Owner decisions, 2026-09-30

Answers to the four earlier open questions.

1. **Pages with nothing to check count as approved on capture only for a tight, verifiable list:** structured exports (FHIR, C-CDA and this app's own JSONL). Native PDF text is **not** on the list, because a PDF page can carry photos alongside its text layer, and the extraction code does not record whether a page has images (✅ `SourceTextPage` in `src/shared/intake-source-text.ts:30-37` has no image field). A native PDF page may join the list only if image presence can be detected and verified. "Approved on capture" must be its own recorded state, not `inspected: true`; that flag means a person looked (`intake-source-text.ts:35`: "No automatic extractor may set inspected=true").
2. **The gate covers measured consumed pages when available**, mapping consumed spans to their page. If a proposal has no measured dependency record, cover every page of its report conservatively. The page the preview opens first comes from the AI's own evidence citation (`#page=N` in a locator's `contentUrl`, read by `intakeEvidencePage` in `src/app/features/intake/ReviewWorkspace.tsx:41-60`). Use it to order and show pages, never as the gate: it is a claim, not a measurement.
3. **Bulk approval is one action.** Approving selected records also approves their pages' text, and the confirmation lists those pages. Agreed.
4. **A text change after acceptance shows "not approved" on the accepted record and lists the page as review work, without blocking anything.** Agreed. The change is narrow, because the rest already exists (✅ checked 2026-09-30):
   - Automatic extraction already refuses to replace reviewed pages: `publishIntakeSourceText` throws `SOURCE_TEXT_REVIEW_CONFLICT`, "Automatic extraction cannot replace reviewed source pages or their relationships" (`src/server/intake-source-text.ts:1019-1023`).
   - A person's own correction already marks the page un-inspected and opens "Inspect the full source page and explicitly confirm it" (`reviewIntakeSourceText`, `correct` action, `intake-source-text.ts:1233-1246`).
   - Only the record-level "not approved" display is new.

- **Proposals are generated before any approval;** approval gates only acceptance. Fine (owner).
- **"By whom" is recorded in the evidence only.** Profiles are never shared: one person per profile, and dependents (people a caregiver manages) do not log in. So the actor is written to the evidence file and JSON, not shown to the caregiver in the app. A caregiver who wants someone else to see records prints the patient's PDF output.

## Requirements

- Each proposal and each accepted clinical record records, separately from its staleness pin: the source revision it was created against (the latest revision at creation time, which may itself be a confirmation), and the verification state of the pages it drew on at that time — approved, approved on capture, or not; when; which revision recorded it; and the actor, in the evidence only (decision 2026-09-30).
- Staleness stays content-only: confirmations never stale a proposal or an accepted record.
- Accepting a clinical record requires approval of every measured consumed page at its current material revision; without measured coverage, require every page of its report. Pages from the approved-on-capture list count as approved. A refused acceptance says which pages still need approval and links to them, with the AI-cited page first. Text approval still never accepts a clinical record.
- Bulk acceptance offers one action that also approves the selected records' unapproved pages, listing them in the confirmation.
- A material text change behind an accepted record shows "not approved" on that record and lists the page as review work. It blocks nothing and does not reopen the record.
- Records accepted before this lands show their pages as not approved and list them as review work; no reconstruction from the revision chain.
- Show the source's verification state wherever a record is reviewed or displayed: the state at creation, and the current state.
- Store this durably and journal it like other record data (`docs/data/record-version-storage.md`): survive a rebuild, and follow the write-amplification rule — small records referencing content by id, never a copy of a whole revision (see [source-text write amplification](../data/record-version-storage.md#source-text-write-amplification)).
- Cover these fictional-fixture scenarios in tests:
  - Confirming a page after a proposal was created does not stale the proposal, but changes the proposal's _currently displayed_ verification state.
  - A proposal created after a confirmation records that confirmation as the state at creation.
  - A material content change after a confirmation stales the proposal (as today), and the old attestation is kept but no longer shown as covering the new content.
  - A structured export (fictional FHIR, C-CDA or JSONL) needs no page approval before acceptance; a native PDF page with a text layer still does.
  - A record on page 3 of a report whose page 1 is unapproved is refused, and the refusal lists page 1.
  - Bulk acceptance with the combined action approves exactly the listed pages and accepts the selected records.
  - A correction to an approved page behind an accepted record shows that record as "not approved" and lists the page, without changing the accepted record.

## Shared source dependency contract

Measured proposal dependencies now cover host-observed pages and spans with related context; [import processing](../import/processing.md#durable-evidence-and-review-boundaries) describes their staleness rule. This ticket adds separate approval facts and UI on top of that retained consumption data.

The shared data contract is the durable `intake_proposal_dependencies:v1:<proposal>` record: source intake IDs, host-observed page numbers or bounded span IDs and their material hashes, plus measured package member relationship and role. A missing record means unknown coverage and keeps the broad source pin. CRS-129 should read this record when present and map a consumed span to its source page for text approval, attaching separate per-page approval facts to the same source/page/hash coordinates. A confirmation revision changes approval history but never the material hash; a later material change stops the earlier approval from covering that page without deleting its attestation. For a multi-record proposal, applying the proposal's observed page or span set to each accepted record is conservative until the host can prove a narrower per-record set; do not infer that set from the model's final citation alone. Keep the approval facts in small durable records, with creation-time and current states distinct, and leave the UI and acceptance gate to this ticket.
