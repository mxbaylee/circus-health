# CRS-129: Record the verified source state behind each proposal

Status: open.

Record, separately from staleness, whether the source text a proposal or accepted clinical record was built from had been checked by a person: which revision, by whom, when, and whether that check still covers today's text.

## Where it stands

Source text has revisions. A _material_ revision changes the text; a _confirmation_ revision records that a person checked a page without changing it. Confirmations are their own revisions in history (`src/server/intake-source-text.ts`), but `sourceTextConfirmationOnly` (`src/server/intake-source-text-dependencies.ts`) keeps the intake's source pin (`src/server/intake-source-pin.ts`, record `intake_source_pin:v1:<intake>`) on the last material revision — a confirmation that resolves an open uncertainty (`resolvedIssueIds`) is excluded from that check and counts as material instead, because it changes what the text claims. Proposals copy the pin (`sourceTextRevisionId`, `sourceTextDependencyToken` in `src/server/intake.ts`), and staleness compares a proposal's pin against the intake's current pin (`assertCurrentProposalSourceText` in `src/server/intake-source-text-dependencies.ts`). Confirming a page therefore never stales existing work. That is correct for staleness: [import processing](../import/processing.md#durable-evidence-and-review-boundaries) documents that "unchanged-text inspection preserves the material dependency."

The gap: nothing records whether the text a proposal or accepted record was built from had actually been checked, which revision recorded that check, who did it or when. Each source-text revision keeps a `review` event with an `actor` and `at` timestamp, and each page carries its own `inspected` flag, but the flag reflects only the current revision's state — reconstructing "was this page confirmed before or after the proposal was created" means walking the revision chain by hand, and is weak under concurrent tabs where a confirmation and a proposal can race.

This item's origin is a review finding (R10): a test in `src/server/test/intake-source-integration.test.ts` ("source corrections stale original/proposal review; identical fresh payload needs a new candidate review") had asserted that a fresh proposal's `sourceTextRevisionId` moves to a confirmation's revision id. That contradicts the pin-to-material rule above and is being corrected separately; it surfaced the same gap this ticket addresses — the test was reaching for a way to say "this proposal knows its text was checked," and the pin is the wrong place to put that.

## General practice this design follows

Medical-records practice keeps content version and attestation as separate facts; this is not a regulatory requirement for this app, but it is worth following. Staleness follows content only. An attestation — who checked a page, when, and what content it covers — is an audit fact tied to a specific content version, not a new content version. It stops covering content once that content changes, but its record is kept rather than discarded. Derived records point at the content version they were made from and can show both the verification state at creation and the current state, the way a lab result can be marked preliminary until verified. FDA 21 CFR Part 11's audit-trail and signature requirements link an attestation to the specific record version it covers, and FHIR's `Provenance` resource targets a specific resource version rather than "the resource" in general; both are reference points for the shape of this design, not requirements this app must meet.

## Personas

There is no persona document yet; [CRS-130](CRS-130.md) writes one. Until then these are inferred from People tags (Family, Professional, Primary Care Provider, Emergency Contact — `docs/features/people-tags.md`), from review being handed between people, and from the owner (the fourth, 2026-09-29):

- Someone keeping their own records with low effort. Confirming a page must not trigger unnecessary re-review of proposals already made from it — churn here is alert fatigue, which lowers safety rather than raising it.
- A caregiver managing a parent's or child's records, handing pages off to family. Needs to know who checked a page and when.
- Someone assembling records for a clinician, a second opinion, or an insurance dispute. Needs to show that a record came from source text that had actually been checked.
- A high-precision tracker. Uploads a large backlog from many sources, down to an eyeglass prescription, then every new record as it arrives — sometimes by re-uploading a full clinical-history export to pick up one new note. Approves pages across a large, growing corpus and needs to see which pages are still unapproved.

Pinning staleness only to the material revision (today's behavior) serves the first persona but gives the others nothing to point at. Moving the pin to confirmations, as the R10 test wrongly expected, would serve them but reintroduce review churn for the first. Keeping both facts, separately, is what serves all of them.

## Owner decisions, 2026-09-29

- **Verified means the page's text was approved.** A person approving a page (**Approve section**, or **Select all shown → Approve text sections**) says its retained text matches the original, so the page is identified and searchable. The purpose is coverage — no unknown unknowns, so a result is never read and then never shown — not a span-level or clinical check.
- **Approval gates acceptance.** A clinical record cannot be accepted until the pages it drew on are approved. The person gets two chances to catch an error: the text, then the record.
- **Records accepted before this lands** are not reconstructed from history. Their pages show as not approved and are offered as review work; approving text is quick next to AI reading, so this gives the person something useful to do while reading runs.

## Requirements

- Each proposal and each accepted clinical record records, separately from its staleness pin: the source revision it was created against (the latest revision at creation time, which may itself be a confirmation), and the verification state of the pages it drew on at that time — verified or not, by whom, when, and which revision recorded it.
- Staleness stays content-only: confirmations never stale a proposal or an accepted record.
- Accepting a clinical record requires every page it drew on to be approved at its current material revision. A refused acceptance says which pages still need approval and links to them. Text approval still never accepts a clinical record.
- Records accepted before this lands show their pages as not approved and list them as review work; no reconstruction from the revision chain.
- Show the source's verification state wherever a record is reviewed or displayed: the state at creation, and the current state.
- Store this durably and journal it like other record data (`docs/data/record-version-storage.md`): survive a rebuild, and follow the write-amplification rule — small records referencing content by id, never a copy of a whole revision (see [source-text write amplification](../data/record-version-storage.md#source-text-write-amplification)).
- Cover these fictional-fixture scenarios in tests:
  - Confirming a page after a proposal was created does not stale the proposal, but changes the proposal's _currently displayed_ verification state.
  - A proposal created after a confirmation records that confirmation as the state at creation.
  - A material content change after a confirmation stales the proposal (as today), and the old attestation is kept but no longer shown as covering the new content.

## Relationship to CRS-118

[CRS-118](CRS-118.md) changes what the staleness pin covers: the specific pages and context each proposal actually consumed, instead of the whole source revision. Page-level verification for exactly those consumed pages is the natural pairing to that work — design the two together rather than separately, since both need the same per-page dependency data.

## Open questions

- Does the gate apply to pages with no OCR to check — native PDF text, structured exports (FHIR, C-CDA, this app's JSONL) — or do those count as approved on capture?
- Which pages did a record draw on? A record whose header is on page 1 and table on page 3 needs both approved. That needs [CRS-118](CRS-118.md)'s per-page consumption data; until then, is the gate the record's own page, or every page of its report?
- In bulk, does approving selected records also approve their pages' text in the same action (with the pages listed in the confirmation), or must text approval come first? The low-effort persona needs one pass; the gate needs the text looked at.
- When a material change un-approves a page behind an accepted record, is showing "not approved" enough, or does the page return to Needs attention?
