## User Story

As a [High-precision tracker](../design/personas.md#build-a-history),
I want a long import to keep making progress and explain delays without repeated intervention,
So that I can build my history while understanding unfinished work and possible repeated charges.

## Plain-English User Walkthrough

1. Upload a history and leave its reading running while continuing other work.
2. If one part keeps timing out or the provider needs sign-in, see the affected source, the reason and honest uncertainty. Other eligible files continue.
3. After service returns, reading continues automatically. If you choose Stop, it remains stopped until your explicit resume; saved work and unknown request outcomes stay visible.

## Acceptance Criteria

### Scenario: Repeated timeouts cool one unit without stopping the job

Given a fictional unit whose requests always time out and other readable units,
When repeated unknown outcomes occur,
Then that unit's counted attempts increase backoff and other units finish,
And there is no attempt cap, pause or exception to clear; a usable response resets cooling and unavailable billing remains explicit.

### Scenario: Provider sign-in returns without another app action

Given imports waiting on authentication,
When an automatic bounded-backoff connection check detects restored sign-in,
Then eligible reading resumes without a dialog click,
And unchanged waiting ticks add no unchanged durable journal entries.

### Scenario: Recover current work and preserve Stop

Given current-format completed-pass baselines, genuinely new capture and a stopped file,
When the runtime is restored with authorization,
Then completed work is not redundantly dispatched, new work is not skipped and Stop remains authoritative,
And queue/wait estimates communicate uncertainty; the remaining correctness and packet-disclosure scenarios below retain their evidence boundaries.

## Decision and why

CRS-126 D2 defines a stall as no progress, and D3 makes Stop the only person-facing pause. The `AGENTS.md` performance rule applies throughout: count work the app controls, and let durable writes scale with what changed.

The earlier requirement to reprocess only the selected stopped file has landed with [automatic recovery](../import/automatic-recovery.md#durable-scheduling). It is no longer open work here.

- **High-precision tracker:** leaves a large backlog running for days. Endless retries, unnoticed waits and growing journals hit them hardest.
- **Low-effort self-tracker:** must not have to find a diagnostics dialog to unblock imports.
- **Receiving clinician:** packets list unread sections today; confirm the list is complete.

**No attempt cap** (owner, 2026-09-30): retries are never capped. Backoff, lower concurrency, a longer request deadline and other cooling are allowed, and anything other than requiring human action is always preferred. This supersedes the earlier plan in requirement 1 to end repeated timeouts in a located exception. [CRS-086](CRS-086.md#prerequisites) keeps "no attempt cap" and points here.

## Requirements

1. **Repeated timeouts on one unit cool down instead of stopping.** With the default 300 s deadline (`CRS_AI_PROXY_TIMEOUT_SECONDS`), a unit whose request always times out is an unknown outcome, and each one authorizes a possibly billed replacement. The grader saw 21 dispatches with no exception and no stall count. Owner, 2026-09-30: there is no attempt cap, ever; cooling is allowed and anything that avoids requiring human action is preferred. So:
   - Count consecutive unknown or timeout outcomes per unit (attempts, not seconds). Each one raises that unit's backoff, and may also lower concurrency for it or automatically lengthen its request deadline above the configured one. Cooling also bounds how often a possibly billed replacement is sent.
   - Other units keep reading.
   - The person sees a non-blocking status that names the unit (original, page range) and may suggest raising `CRS_AI_PROXY_TIMEOUT_SECONDS`. They never have to act: no stop, no exception to clear, no Resume.
   - A usable response on the unit resets its cooling.
   - Test: an always-timing-out fictional unit is retried with growing backoff, other units finish, the status names the unit, and no exception or pause is raised.

2. **Detect sign-in without a click.** The prerequisite key (`modelRecoveryKey`, `src/server/model-bridge.ts:101`) changes only on configuration change or a successful test in the model connection dialog; a sign-in at the provider behind LiteLLM is invisible. While imports wait on authentication, run the fictional connection check automatically on a capped backoff, and continue reading when it succeeds. Test that reading resumes without a click. Then soften the notice wording again.
3. **No journal write on an unchanged wait.** The authentication wait writes a `provider-prerequisite-wait` entry every 30 s with nothing changed (10 writes in 10 ticks; about 2,880 a day per waiting item). Keep the unchanged deadline in memory, or back off the check, so durable writes happen only when state changes.

## Owner decisions needed

- **Unknown outcomes and billing** (carried from CRS-135): LiteLLM or provider idempotency could let a retry fetch the original result instead of paying again. Should an available reconciliation mechanism be required before a replacement request, and what should routes without one do? Requirement 1's cooling bounds how often replacements are sent, but not whether each one is billed. Whatever the answer, it adds no attempt cap, and an answer that needs no human action is preferred (owner, 2026-09-30).
- **Stop on an item already `review_ready` with paused reading** (carried from CRS-135): a new reprocess operation now reopens corrected completed work, preserves exact replay, and resumes only the selected stopped file. Decide whether Stop on an otherwise current `review_ready` item with paused reading should offer Resume for that unfinished reading without requiring a source correction. The old early-proposal capture stopgap has been removed; see [automatic recovery](../import/automatic-recovery.md#durable-scheduling).
- **Exceptions that live only in source text**, not in the reading plan, may be missing from packet `readingGaps` (suspected). Should packets list them too? Brief mode lists none; is that intended?
